# Week 3: Become an AI FDE

What each day of Week 3 covers, so you can answer the student's questions about it: what the slides teach, what the labs and the product build are for, what to watch out for, and corrections to the videos. Read `guides/for_agents/AGENT_COURSE_GUIDE.md` first, for what runs through the whole course.

The week's learning objectives:
- Lead AI initiatives with your clients from discovery and metrics to governance, responsible AI and handover.
- Secure agents against prompt injection and the lethal trifecta with guardrails, policies and AI gateways.
- Create a data and integration architecture for agents with an enterprise context layer and MCP gateway.
- Deploy LangGraph agents to production with AWS Bedrock AgentCore Runtime, Gateway, Policy and Memory.

AWS calls it Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, and the course says AWS Bedrock AgentCore for the same product. Model calls stay on OpenRouter all week.

## Week 3 Day 1: AI Solutions Architect

- **Slides:** the agent development lifecycle (the ADLC's first meaning) in five phases: discovery, R&D, development, implementation and maintenance, with ownership, governance, evals and change management throughout, and the FDE's work in each. Discovery sorts use cases into automation, acceleration and differentiation, with three questions for each.
- **Slides:** systems architecture (gateways, integrations and the context layer; start simple and expect a redesign within a year), and where an agent runs: your own infrastructure, a managed runtime like AgentCore, or the model lab's own. Governance with tollgates, autonomy as a risk decision for each action, and "treat agents like any other software asset".
- **Slides:** responsible AI's six pillars, privacy with LLM providers, accountability, handover (the client runs it without the FDE) and adoption. Udemy's Role Play with UnderCover's head of risk and compliance comes after this.
- **Lab** (`week3day1`): set up AWS for the week. An account on the Paid plan, MFA on root, us-east-1, IAM access to billing and a $10 monthly budget with alerts, the `undercover-course` policy and group with an IAM user `undercover` and its access key, the AWS CLI with `aws configure`, then checks from Python with boto3. Nothing today uses AgentCore or costs anything. The free route, `week3day1free`, sets up a Render account.
- **Watch out for:** use us-east-1 wherever the student lives; if something seems missing in the console, check the region selector first. For the budget, follow the notebook's Step 4 and exclude Credit and Refund, which the video passes over. A budget that counts credits shows zero and never alerts. AWS's screens change, so follow the notebook where they differ from the video.
- **Costs:** AWS has no hard spending cap, and the student is responsible for what they use; a waiver of accidental charges isn't guaranteed. The Render route and watching Ed are the alternatives.
- **Corrections:** IAM is Identity and Access Management. New customers get the same credits on either plan, $100 and up to another $100, and the course needs the Paid plan because the Free plan can't use Bedrock AgentCore. Paying doesn't by itself stop a provider training on your data, so check the terms and settings of the product you use.
- **Assignment** (in the free notebook, for either route): a one-page discovery note on a business problem, with success criteria, a baseline and a target, and as a stretch, an architecture decision record.

## Week 3 Day 2: Security, Guardrails, API Gateways

- **Slides:** three kinds of risk: what an agent can do where it runs, what it can do through its APIs, and prompt injection. Simon Willison's lethal trifecta is private data, untrusted content and the ability to communicate externally. Four kinds of guardrails.
- **Slides:** API, MCP and model gateways and the products in each, with Ed's advice to stay lightweight while they change. The AgentCore suite, and how AgentCore Gateway turns an API into MCP tools, checks a Cedar Policy and adds the target's credentials.
- **Lab 1** (`week3day2_lab1`): CLM-1901 hides an instruction to approve a $4,800 watch. GPT-6 Luna declines it and GPT-4o mini approves it. A keyword check in `before_agent` middleware catches it until the instruction is reworded. A model check with Structured Outputs catches both versions, and the lab tests it for false alarms on the 25 ordinary claims.
- **Build** (SECURE): an API key for the agent (`UNDERCOVER_API_KEY`, sent as `X-API-Key`), Lab 1's check as the first node of the claim graph (a flagged claim is referred), an OpenAPI document of the agent's eight operations for the Gateway, and the app packaged for Lambda with Mangum. The graph's approval pause comes out.
- **Lab 2** (`week3day2_lab2`): deploy the app (the backend and dashboard) to Lambda with a Function URL. Create a Gateway with the app as its target and the key in AgentCore Identity, and call the tools from the notebook. Then add a Cedar Policy forbidding `pay_claim` over $1,000, first log-only, then enforced. The free route, `week3day2_lab2free`, deploys the app on Render.
- **Watch out for:** the build adds `UNDERCOVER_API_KEY` to `.env`, so a student on Ed's branch has none until they add it. Without it Lab 2 stops with a `KeyError` and the agent's service won't start. Letters and numbers are safest, and `uv run python -c "import secrets; print(secrets.token_hex(32))"` prints one; offer to add it to `.env` for the student without printing it. The README and both Lab 2s give the same step. The agent stays on the laptop today, so the deployed dashboard can't start dispatches yet. The teardown keeps the Lambda app for Day 3, and `DELETE_THE_APP = True` removes it too. `uv run monitor.py` shows AWS spend but doesn't replace the budget alerts.
- **Corrections:** the hook in Lab 1 is `before_agent`, where Ed says `before_model`. The last architecture slide puts "Wording, manual and notes" in Memory, but the wording and manual go into the context layer and Memory holds the agent's notes on each customer. No set of checks makes an agent bulletproof. The Function URL is public: the key protects the agent's routes but doesn't stop anyone calling the function.
- **Assignments:** fool the model check, then improve it; write an `after_model` hook; and add a Cedar policy refusing an approval over $5,000.

## Week 3 Day 3: Enterprise Context Layer & Integrations

- **Slides:** from context engineering to the enterprise context layer, in four parts: what the data means, how the work is done, what the agent may see and do, and where it came from. Most of it is long-standing data governance, with Atlan's definition and Gartner's note on master data management.
- **Slides:** the gateway and three sources of data. The gateway holds the security team's rules, the context layer holds data the business teams own (read as tools), memory holds the agent's own notes on each customer, and the database is the system of record, reached through the app's API. Surveys of context layer and memory products, AgentCore Memory's strategies and namespaces, and questions for enterprise integrations.
- **Build** (CONTEXT): the agent's tools go through the Gateway when `UNDERCOVER_GATEWAY_URL` is set. The context layer becomes its own FastMCP server over seven files in `context/`, each naming its source, owner and review date. Every decision gets the full record that section 14 of the claims manual asks for. AgentCore Memory keeps facts and claim summaries for each customer when `UNDERCOVER_MEMORY_ID` is set, and the claim page shows the lookups and memory in a "Context used" panel.
- **Lab** (`week3day3`): update the app on Lambda and deploy the context server as a second Lambda behind IAM auth. Create a Gateway with both as targets and the Policy enforced, then try lookups and two refusals. Create a Memory, then work Oliver Grant's giraffe claim (CLM-1005) and coconut claim (CLM-1006), where the agent can recall the first.
- **Watch out for:** `npm run dev` now also starts the context server at http://127.0.0.1:8006/mcp. The agent decides for itself whether to search its memory, so CLM-1006 may show no memory step. Memories are extracted in the background, usually within two minutes. Document search matches the whole phrase, so "Trip Interruption" misses "Trip Cancellation and Interruption". Ed stops showing Langfuse from today, though the tracing code is still there.
- **Watch out for:** the free route runs the context layer on the laptop with the app on Render, without the Gateway or Memory. The teardown keeps both Lambda functions for Day 4, and `DELETE_THE_FUNCTIONS = True` removes them too.
- **Corrections:** `UNDERCOVER_API_KEY` is the agent's key, which the agent or the Gateway sends and the backend checks; it never goes in a frontend setting. A memory search covers everything in the current customer's namespace, and the claim ID only leaves out the current claim's own summary.
- **Assignment:** add AgentCore's Episodic strategy and compare it with the facts and summaries. On the free route, a customer memory in Supabase with LangGraph's `PostgresStore` and pgvector.

## Week 3 Day 4: Production Deployment

- **Slides:** configuring an agent for production: settings, secrets in AgentCore Identity, who may call the agent, limits (a 60-second idle timeout), logs and cost. Where an agent runs, revisited. The AgentCore CLI's `create`, `dev`, `deploy`, `invoke` and `remove all`, with a zip build and no Docker.
- **Slides:** one Runtime session runs a whole dispatch, the dispatcher and up to 25 claim agents, and answers at once while the work carries on. A sent-back claim resumes in a new session from its checkpoints in Supabase.
- **Lab 1** (`week3day4_lab1`): install the CLI at the pinned version, `npm install -g @aws/agentcore@1.0.0-rc.5`, and deploy a hello world. Run a small LangGraph agent with `agentcore dev`, deploy it and compare a cold and a warm session. Then pause CLM-9001 in one session and resume it in another with a handler's note that the taxi receipt is in twice.
- **Build** (RUNTIME): the approval pause returns, with Sign off (the app pays) and Send back with a note (the agent decides again). The agent's service becomes an AgentCore app, `agent/entrypoint.py`, that runs the same on the laptop (port 8080) and on Runtime. The app keeps one run at a time, with a lock in the database and a heartbeat from the agent.
- **Lab 2** (`week3day4_lab2`): bring back Day 3's pieces, deploy the agent to Runtime and point the app at it. From the deployed dashboard, run a swarm of 25, sign off one claim and send CLM-1011 back. The teardown deletes the CDK bootstrap and keeps the two Lambda functions. The free route, `week3day4_lab2free`, runs the agent as a second Render service.
- **Watch out for:** on `main` or the student's own branch, Lab 1's two `git mv` commands move `runtime/` and `agentcore/` from `setup/setup_week3day4/` to the root; on `week3day4-start` they're already there. `agentcore dev` and the agent both use port 8080, so stop one before starting the other. The first deploy creates the CDK bootstrap with a KMS key at $1 a month, which Lab 2 deletes.
- **Watch out for:** if `npm run dev` restarts in the middle of a dispatch, new starts are refused for up to five minutes. `npm run seed` clears that once the old work has stopped, but it also resets the queue.
- **Corrections:** 60 seconds is an idle timeout, and a session doing registered work stays alive for up to 8 hours. `npm ci` is clean install. In Lab 1, the start of the resumed reply is the original proposal, so print `reply["decision"]` for the new one. A sign-off pays without asking the agent, then a short session lets the agent finish the claim's graph and save it to Memory.
- **Assignment:** before the teardown, run a held-out evaluation from the deployed dashboard and compare its time and cost with the laptop's.

## Week 3 Day 5: Capstone

- **Slides:** the UnderCover Copilot, an agent for claims handlers in the dashboard's sidebar. Its conversations are durable (checkpoints in Supabase, and it asks before paying), it runs on its own AgentCore Runtime with a session per conversation, and AgentCore Memory keeps a namespace for each handler. It acts with the handler's own sign-in, never the claim agent's key.
- **Slides:** the assignment, the final teardown and the close: the skills, the 12 learning objectives, FDE job postings, and the next step: real-world experience, starting with the assignment and a problem for a real user.
- **Build** (COPILOT): Ed builds it with Claude Code after Codex reviews the plan; there's no lab for the build. It's a hand-written LangGraph loop with tools for claims, customers, work and the context layer, a confirmation card before any payment, waiting in code for a dispatch, a success-criteria check worked out in code, and handler memory. Locally it runs on port 8081 beside the claim agent on 8080.
- **Lab 1** (`week3day5_lab1`): deploy everything to AWS, with two runtimes and two Memories. Then the handover conversation as Dana: work the queue with 10 agents, ask why CLM-1011 needs her, send it back with a note, sign off another payout, and ask whether the success criteria agreed on Week 1 Day 2 are met. The free route, `week3day5_lab1free`, runs the Copilot as a third Render service with a shorter handover.
- **Lab 2** (`week3day5_lab2`): the final teardown of everything on AWS, including both Lambda functions, then three checks: UnderCover's resources by name, everything Resource Explorer finds in us-east-1, and the cost from the budgets.
- **Assignment:** a LangGraph solution for a business problem with decisions within limits, a variation of UnderCover or something completely different. It needs human sign-off above the limit, database checkpoints, a context layer, memory for each user and runtime deployment. Decide how to measure success before building, and share it as a PR with a short write-up and a link to the repo.
- **Watch out for:** in Lab 2 Part 2, run the three terminal commands (`agentcore remove all --yes`, `agentcore deploy --yes` and `git restore agentcore/agentcore.json`) before the cell that clears `.env.local`; the video doesn't mention them. A new AWS account has default resources, so the account-wide list won't be empty. Budget figures update up to three times a day, so check again over the next day or two, and deactivate the IAM user's access key when finished with AWS.
- **Corrections:** straight through in the success criteria counts every claim not referred, including approvals waiting for sign-off. The claim agent's memory is organized by customer and the Copilot's by handler. When Ed says he's asking Copilot for a second opinion on the plan, he means Codex.
